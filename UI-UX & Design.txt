Activity 2:
what’s wrong with it
the websites look super outdated, like something from the 90s. plain white backgrounds, default blue and purple links, no real layout or structure.


there’s almost no branding besides maybe a logo. everything feels really plain and kind of thrown together.


all the links look exactly the same: same color, same font, no way to tell what’s important.


no navigation bar or search box, so you just get this massive wall of links.


there’s no spacing or headings to break things up. it’s just text on texy.


color contrast isn’t great either. Those default purple links on white are hard to read, especially for people with vision issues.


it doesn’t seem to adjust at all for mobile screens. On a phone or tablet, you’d have to zoom in just to see anything.


and in one of the examples (for example, in the 3rd pic), there are way too many colors and fonts fighting for attention. it’s just visual chaos.





how i’d fix it
start with a simple, clean grid,  like a header at the top, sidebar for navigation, and main content area.


add a clear navigation bar with dropdowns so people can actually find stuff.


use headings, icons, and some space between sections so it doesn’t feel so cramped.


turn plain links into proper buttons or add hover effects so you know what’s clickable.


make sure links have actual descriptions instead of “click here.”


add a search bar and maybe some filtering or a little dashboard to make browsing easier.


keep things minimal but tweak the spacing and color contrast so it’s easier on the eyes.


and of course, make the whole thing responsive so it looks good on phones too.




Activity 3:

Chosen App: Spotify
For someone who’s colorblind
so the main issue is that spotify uses green for almost everything: play button, progress bar, active icons. if you can’t see green well, half the UI just looks the same.
to fix that, i’d probably:
add more contrast between buttons and the background (not just rely on color).


use symbols or text next to color changes. like, if something’s “active,” maybe give it a check mark or a slightly different shape.


have a “high contrast mode” in settings that boosts brightness and outlines key buttons better.
or someone with shaky hands
tiny buttons are the worst. i’d make the interface a bit more forgiving.
make buttons and sliders bigger and further apart. it’s easy to accidentally tap skip when you meant pause.


add a “steady mode” so actions like deleting or skipping tracks need a double tap or a confirm pop up.


support voice control: “next song,” “pause,” “volume up.”


let users customize layout — like moving their most-used buttons to the middle of the screen.




Activity 4:
user journey: ordering food on swiggy
open the app – i grab my phone, open swiggy, and wait for it to load.
feeling: fine, maybe a bit impatient if it takes too long.


browse restaurants – i scroll through the list, check ratings, prices, and delivery times.
frustration: sometimes too many choices, or the filters don’t really narrow things down well.
happy: when i find a place nearby with good reviews and short delivery time.


look at the menu – open the restaurant and start scrolling through their dishes.
frustration: some menus are messy or have bad photos, so i’m not sure what i’m getting.
happy: when there are clear pictures and quick add-to-cart buttons.


add food to cart – i pick what i want, maybe customize something (like no onions, extra cheese).
frustration: if customization options aren’t clear or don’t save properly.
happy: when the app remembers my usual order or gives me a good combo suggestion.


checkout and payment – go to the cart, double-check items, and choose payment method.
frustration: if the promo code doesn’t work or there’s some hidden delivery charge at the end.
happy: when the payment is smooth and quick (like with UPI or saved card).


order confirmed / waiting – i get the confirmation screen with the estimated delivery time and live tracking.
happy: watching the little map update with the driver moving toward me.
frustration: when the ETA keeps changing or the driver goes the wrong way.


food arrives – the delivery person shows up, i get my food, and hopefully it’s hot.
happy: everything’s right, and the food looks like the pictures.
frustration: wrong order, cold food, or missing items.
